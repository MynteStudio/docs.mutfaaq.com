title: Allergens

Declaring allergens helps customers identify products that may not be suitable for them. Add allergens to each relevant product and keep the information up to date whenever a recipe changes.

<Warning>
  Allergen information is provided as guidance and does not guarantee the absence of cross-contamination. Customers with an allergy should speak directly with the restaurant.
</Warning>

## Add allergens to a product

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the product">
    From **Edit menu**, select the product you want to update.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the allergens">
    In **General information**, open **Allergens** and select every allergen associated with the product.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the product">
    Select **Save**. The allergens appear in the product details on the customer menu.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Mutfaaq provides a standardized list of 14 allergens: gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soy, milk, nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphites, lupin, and molluscs.

The names are automatically displayed in the customer’s menu language. You select from the provided list and cannot create custom allergen labels.

## Understand the customer filter

When at least one product in the menu declares an allergen, customers can open **Filter by allergens** and choose what they want to avoid.

The filter is named **Hide products containing**. It removes every product that declares at least one selected allergen.

<Tip>
  For example, if a customer selects **Peanuts** and **Milk**, Mutfaaq hides products that declare peanuts, milk, or both.
</Tip>

The filter only changes what is displayed on the customer’s device. It does not modify the menu or the products themselves.

## Keep allergen information reliable

- Confirm the information with the people who prepare the products.
- Review allergens whenever ingredients, recipes, or suppliers change.
- Select every relevant allergen from the list.
- Do not treat a missing allergen label as proof that a product is allergen-free.

Mutfaaq does not distinguish between direct presence and possible traces. Use the information to describe known allergens, while handling cross-contamination guidance directly with your customers.
